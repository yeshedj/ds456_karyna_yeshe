Description

Aphasia is a language disorder that affects a person’s ability to communicate, typically resulting from brain damage due to a stroke or traumatic brain injury (TBI). Symptoms of aphasia can manifest in various ways, such as difficulty with speech production, comprehension, reading, or writing. There is a wide range of severity levels that makes it a complex condition to study and treat.

Motivation

  • Large Language Models (LLMs) perform poorly for individuals with speech and language difficulties
  • AI models are unable to accurately analyze impaired speech patterns due to being almost exclusively trained on neurotypical, fluent adults (Standard HAI 2026).

Research Question

RQ: How do current AI models perform when processing aphasic speech across symptom severity levels, where and why do they fail?

Ethical Issues

  • Data privacy?: All the audio samples and human transcriptions are being used from the restricted-access research database AphasiaBank. This makes sure that institutional agreements and policies in regards to data sharing and participant anonymity is preserved.
  • Who may benefit?: When speech recognition models are trained and evaluated only on healthy, fluent speech, individuals with speech impairments are unable to access assistive tools that could significantly improve their quality of life. Building models that can accurately process disordered speech is important for making sure individuals with speech difficulties are not excluded from the benefits of modern technology.Training speech recognition models on aphasic speech samples can improve inclusivity and accessibility within AI system development.

    ref_words = sum(ref_words),
    errors = sum(substitutions + deletions + insertions),
    pooled_wer = errors / ref_words
  )

# Prepare data for the plot
plot_data <- df %>%
  mutate(file = factor(file, levels = file[order(wer)])) %>%
  pivot_longer(
    cols = c(substitutions, deletions, insertions),
    names_to = "error_type",
    values_to = "errors"
  ) %>%
  mutate(rate = errors / ref_words)

# Plot WER by recording
ggplot(plot_data, aes(x = rate, y = file, fill = error_type)) +

COMPLETED STEPS

Step 1: Baseline & Prep

  • Isolated the “Cinderella Task” to keep the context consistent across all samples
  • Selected a control group of healthy speakers from the database to map standard speech performance which will serve as a comparison baseline against aphasic speech samples
  • Trimmed audio files to only include the ‘Cinderella Task’ on this control set (via GarageBand)
  • Cleaned transcripts to only include sound utterances, removing any documented physical gestures

Step 2: Running Model on Control set

  • Selected OpenAI’s WhisperAI as our transcription model for analyzing speech
  • Ran WhisperAI on the healthy control audio files to generate machine-made transcripts

NEXT STEPS

Step 3: Creating Rulebook

  • Define criteria for what constitutes a “good” or “acceptable” transcript
  • WhisperAI control vs. Human control: Then using these rules, evaluate WhisperAI’s performance against the human-made transcripts to establish its baseline performance on non-aphasic/healthy speech

Step 4: Initial Comparison

  • Evaluate the transcripts generated by WhisperAI against the human-made transcripts using the rules created in Step 3
  • Document any errors or patterns observed in WhisperAI’s performance on this initial control group doing the ‘Cinderella Task’
  • Create a summary of the results

Step 5: Iterative Run on Aphasic Samples

  • Select non-healthy/aphasic speech samples doing the ‘Cinderella Task’ across varying severity levels in the AphasiaBank dataset to analyze
  • Create a trimmed audio set OR automate by writing a script
  • Feed this into WhisperAI to generate transcripts for the ‘Cinderaella Task’ aphasic speech samples

Step 6: Evaluation Across Groups/Transcription Methods

  • Evaluate the performance of the various generated transcripts using the rubric created in Step 3
    • Human vs. WhisperAI:
      • Aphasic Human-made Transcript vs. Aphasic WhisperAI Transcript: to test the model’s accuracy on impaired/disordered speech
      • Control Human-made Transcript vs. Control WhisperAI Transcript: to test the model’s baseline accuracy on standard speech patterns/usage
    • Group vs. Group:
      • Aphasic WhisperAI Transcript vs. Control WhisperAI Transcript: isolates errors to see if WhisperAI makes more or different types of errors (i.e., dropped word, hallucinations, misinterpretations, etc.) when processing the aphasic speech sample compared to the control speech group
      • Aphasic Human Transcript vs. Control Human Transcript: to grasp a baseline of the clinical differences in the speech samples before any involvement with the models

Step 7: Overall Evaluation & Visualization

  • Evaluate performance differences between WhisperAI and Human transcriptions, and also between the control and aphasic sample groups using the rulebook criteria from Step 3
  • Create visualizations to illustrate patterns/differences in performance across the various groups and transcription methods
